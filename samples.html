  • Procedural Hit Reactions Additive reactions from hit location and direction across different skeletons.

    Hit Location And Direction

    I built these hit reactions entirely in Control Rig, with zero baked hit-reaction animation. The rig calculates additive rotations from hit location and direction. The struck area responds most strongly, with rotation falling off along the skeletal hierarchy.

    One Control Rig, Multiple Skeletons

    I reused one Control Rig across humanoid and creature skeletons. It imports each character’s skeleton and calculates reactions from its hierarchy, so the same hit-reaction logic works across different body shapes.

  • Humanoid Rigging Maya rigging with helper bones and RBF deformation carried into Unreal.

    Rig In Motion

    I built this humanoid rig in Maya, from animator controls to deformation helpers. I test the skinned skeleton in motion to check how the shoulders, elbows and hips hold up through changing poses.

    IK/FK Controls

    I built switchable IK/FK arm controls, pole vectors and individual finger controls. The hand can be positioned directly in IK or the arm posed joint by joint in FK.

    Helper Bones And Quaternion Math

    I use corrective and roll bones to support joint volume and distribute limb twist. Custom Python nodes in Maya use quaternion operations to calculate the helper-bone rotations.

    RBF Pose-Space Deformation

    I author RBF (radial basis function) setups with Epic’s Pose Wrangler. The solvers blend corrective helper-bone poses as the driving joints rotate, preserving the shoulder shape across raised and lowered arm poses.

    Unreal Post-Process AnimBP

    I export the Pose Wrangler RBF setup to Unreal and evaluate the corrections in a post-process Animation Blueprint, after the main animation pose.

    The shoulder test toggles postprocessing on and off; the hip test checks the helper bones under large leg rotations.
